# PART IV

## THE NEW ENGINEER

*The role changes when execution is no longer proof of value*

The future engineer is not the last person at the keyboard. They are the person who designs what the system is allowed to become.

This is not a consolation chapter about acquiring generic new skills. It is a harder description of professional work: establishing real bounds, preserving interventions that can change an outcome, governing permissions, and ensuring that a system can still explain why it acted when the incident has passed.

::: {.part-map}
**In this part**

- **14 — Design the Bounds or Become Obsolete**: the limits that give a role continuing force.
- **15 — The Human in the Loop Is Not Enough**: the difference between witnessing and intervening.
- **16 — The Engineer as Governor**: technical work as the design of rules, authority, and exceptions.
- **17 — Systems That Remember Why They Acted**: decision memory as operational infrastructure.
- **18 — The Last Job Was Never About Typing Commands**: the identity that remains when execution becomes abundant.
:::

> The work that survives is not the work a system cannot perform. It is the work an organisation cannot afford to leave unowned.

The book ends by returning to the question that began it: what should still wait for us?
