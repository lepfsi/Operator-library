# PART III

## WHAT REMAINS HUMAN

*Not a residue of tasks, but a burden of consequence*

The human role is not whatever the system cannot yet do. It is what someone must still answer for.

This part refuses vague claims about intuition, empathy, or context. It names the operational burdens that do not transfer merely because a task does: choosing among plausible harms, carrying responsibility, noticing the relationship behind an exception, deciding when a permission should not be used.

::: {.part-map}
**In this part**

- **09 — Judgment Is Not a Prediction**: the difference between ranking an outcome and owning a choice.
- **10 — Responsibility Has a Name**: the person behind a distributed decision.
- **11 — Context Is the Missing Interface**: why context is a relationship, not a missing field.
- **12 — Trust Is a Permission**: the organisational act hidden inside delegation.
- **13 — The Courage to Do Nothing**: why restraint becomes work when action is cheap.
:::

> What remains human is not a small remainder. It is the place where a consequence still needs an owner.

The final part asks how to design a profession around that obligation.
